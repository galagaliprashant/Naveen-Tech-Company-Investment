# Business Plan: Staged Technology Services, Academy & Managed FDE Company

**Prepared for:** Naveen and family, prospective promoter-investors  
**Prepared by:** Prashanth, Founder — Parambhaav  
**Location:** Bengaluru, India  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Status:** Investment discussion draft — subject to legal, tax and financial diligence

## 1. Investment decision summary

This proposal is to establish a Bengaluru-based technology company funded through a **₹5 crore, 24-month, milestone-controlled capital envelope**. The company will start as a revenue-led digital-transformation services business, pilot an FDE training academy only after the services operation is stable, and later offer managed Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) services.

This is deliberately **not** a proposal to spend ₹5 crore building a broad enterprise-AI platform before customers are proven. The company will first deliver paid client outcomes; it will build reusable software accelerators only where at least three paying clients demonstrate the same recurring problem.

Management represents that three existing Parambhaav clients are signed or paying and together represent **more than ₹1 crore of Year-1 opportunity**. This statement must be validated by executed contracts, invoices, payment evidence, scope, billing schedules, and client consent before it is used as the financial baseline.

The investment decision should be conditional on:

1. Written agreement on ownership, Board composition, CEO equity, and funding instrument.
2. Legal transfer or novation of the three client engagements where applicable.
3. A contract-backed Year-1 operating model, approved before Tranche 2.
4. A written delegation of authority, bank controls, and tranche-release process.

## 2. Company proposition

### Phase 1 — Digital Transformation & Digital Growth Services

The company will sell packaged, outcome-led engagements to Indian SMEs, growth-stage companies, and mid-market organisations. Initial buyers are founders, COOs, Heads of Sales, and Heads of Digital who need to modernise customer journeys, internal workflows, and reporting.

Core offerings:

- Websites, web applications, Android/iOS applications, and custom software.
- Workflow digitisation, CRM/ERP integrations, dashboards, analytics, and AI-enabled automation.
- Ongoing application support and improvement.
- Digital Growth Services: performance marketing, content, campaign reporting, and limited project-led PR support.

Digital Growth Services are a client-retention and cross-sell capability, rather than a separate reason to invest. It must operate with its own job costing and client contracts so that the company can see whether it is profitable.

**Market position:** the company will not market itself as a generic low-cost development agency. Its positioning is: *a delivery partner that combines software, automation, and measurable digital-growth outcomes for growing businesses.* Each initial offering must have a defined scope, timeline, outcome, exclusions, acceptance criteria, and change-control process.

### Phase 2 — FDE Academy pilot

The FDE Academy is a **Year-2 pilot**, not a Year-1 revenue assumption. It may offer paid, practical training for graduates and early-career engineers in full-stack engineering, cloud, AI-assisted development, client communication, and project delivery.

It may start only when all of the following are true:

- A curriculum and assessment rubric are approved.
- A qualified trainer and senior technical mentor are available.
- A paid pilot cohort has been recruited without relying on unsubstantiated placement promises.
- Refund, attendance, data privacy, certificate, and grievance policies are in place.
- The Board approves the academy budget and success measures.

Academy income must be reported separately from services revenue. It is not treated as dependable recurring income until multiple paid cohorts have completed with acceptable completion, satisfaction, and job-readiness evidence.

### Phase 3 — Managed FDE services

In this proposal, an FDE is a company-employed engineer who works closely with a client while remaining managed by the company. The client buys an accountable dedicated engineer or managed squad, with agreed deliverables, service levels, supervision, replacement coverage, and reporting.

The company will not present short-course graduates as client-ready FDEs. FDE deployment requires demonstrated engineering skill, client communication, domain understanding, and senior technical supervision. The first deployment should be an experienced engineer or a senior-led pod, only against a paid client requirement.

## 3. Product-development discipline

The company will not commit to a broad “enterprise AI engineering platform” in its first 24 months. That category is too broad for a new company without a proven buyer, data access, technical leadership bench, or product-market evidence.

Instead, the delivery team will maintain a reusable-asset register: integrations, workflow templates, dashboard components, deployment patterns, and domain-specific automations created during paid work. A product investment proposal may be made only when:

1. Three or more paying clients have the same material problem.
2. The buyer, problem, workflow, data requirements, and security requirements are documented.
3. The company can demonstrate that a reusable product will improve gross margin, speed, or recurring revenue.
4. The Board approves a separate product budget, delivery owner, and success metrics.

Sector-level AI market growth is not proof of demand for this company. It is background only. The NASSCOM–BCG report projects India’s AI market at approximately US$17–22 billion by 2027, but the company must validate its own customer demand and willingness to pay. |---|---|---|---|
| Promoter / investor | Naveen and family | Capital, shareholder approvals, strategic oversight | Does not run daily operations unless separately appointed to an executive role |
| Director | Manjunath | Board governance, finance oversight, office/admin, HR policy, compliance coordination | Approves matters reserved to the Board; does not direct product or client delivery outside the agreed operating process |

| Advisor | Prasanna Kumar | Planning, process, and governance advice when requested | No executive or Board authority unless separately appointed |

### Avoiding the Manjunath–Prashanth operating conflict

Manjunath and Prashanth have separate domains. The CEO owns the operating plan and performance of every business line. Manjunath owns governance, finance oversight, office/admin controls, and HR policy. Non-technical staff may work functionally with Manjunath, but their priorities, resourcing, and performance objectives must align with the CEO-approved operating plan.

The Board must adopt a delegation matrix before the first tranche. At minimum, it must define CEO spending and hiring limits, contract approval limits, bank signatories, related-party approval, reporting, and tranche certification.

## 5. Questions to be agreed with the investor

The following questions are intentionally open. They must be answered in writing before incorporation and before any investment is transferred.

1. Who will hold shares at incorporation: Naveen alone, named family members, or an entity/trust?
2. What exact shareholding, voting rights, and equity class will each shareholder hold?
3. What equity will Prashanth receive in addition to his ₹60 lakh annual salary, and what vesting, performance, dilution, and exit terms apply?
4. Who will be the second director? Will Naveen serve on the Board?
5. Who can appoint or remove the CEO, approve annual budgets, issue new shares, or approve future fundraising?
6. Is the ₹5 crore a binding capital commitment, or may funding stop after any tranche?
7. Will each tranche be funded as equity, preference shares, shareholder loan, or a documented combination?
8. What valuation and future-dilution framework applies? Is Naveen expected to own 100% initially?
9. Which decisions require investor/Board approval, and which are delegated to the CEO?
10. What reporting frequency, audit rights, bank-account controls, and access to records will the investor receive?

The legal structure must be confirmed by an Indian Company Secretary and legal counsel. A private company requires at least two directors, and at least one director must satisfy the resident-director requirement. ## 6. Governance, ethics and controls

Before the company accepts capital, it will adopt:

- A shareholders’ agreement and Articles of Association addressing ownership, transfer restrictions, dilution, reserved matters, founder/CEO exit, and dispute resolution.
- A CEO employment or engagement agreement, including ₹60 lakh annual fixed compensation, equity terms if approved, confidentiality, IP assignment, performance expectations, and termination terms.
- A related-party, ethics, and arm’s-length transactions policy. Any work involving Parambhaav, investors, directors, relatives, or connected parties must be documented, priced on arm’s-length terms, disclosed, and approved under the applicable process.
- A monthly management-information pack and quarterly Board review.
- A bank-control policy: company account only, dual approval for payments above the Board-approved threshold, and no personal use of company funds.

## 7. ₹5 crore funding plan

The ₹5 crore is a Board-approved funding envelope. It is not an unrestricted lump-sum transfer and each release is subject to evidence and Board approval.

| Tranche | Indicative period | Amount | Required evidence before release |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1. Establish | Months 1–3 | ₹75 lakh | Incorporation, company bank account, governance documents, client-transfer plan, initial team plan, approved 90-day budget |
| 2. Deliver | Months 4–9 | ₹1.25 crore | Active delivery on signed work, monthly MIS, client-satisfaction evidence, approved six-person staffing plan, updated cash forecast |
| 3. Repeat | Months 10–15 | ₹1.25 crore | Repeatable services pipeline, demonstrated contribution margin, a paid managed-FDE pilot/requirement, academy curriculum and feasibility evidence |
| 4. Scale selectively | Months 16–24 | ₹1.75 crore | Board-approved scale plan, revenue-performance review, academy paid-pilot evidence, and paid managed-FDE demand |
| **Total** | **24 months** | **₹5 crore** | **Funding remains milestone-controlled** |

Missing a release gate does not automatically trigger the next tranche. The Board may delay, reduce, redirect, or decline the release based on the evidence, subject to the final investment documentation.

### Office approach

Begin with a six-seat managed or flexible office; do not sign a conventional long-term lease in Year 1. A ₹12,000 per-seat monthly planning cap, plus controlled meeting-room and setup provision, is appropriate for the budget ceiling. Managed-office listings in Bengaluru show a wide range and cite a typical quoted rate around ₹8,499 per seat/month; actual premises must be selected through competitive quotes. [Bengaluru managed-office listing](https://instaoffice.in/listings/managed-office/in/bengaluru)

Move to a dedicated larger office only when all three conditions hold: at least 12 regular in-office users, six months of visible delivery demand, and Board approval of the total occupancy cost including deposit, fit-out, utilities, and lock-in exposure.

### Critical cost observation

Prashanth’s stated fixed salary alone is ₹1.20 crore over 24 months—24% of the total ₹5 crore funding envelope. The remaining personnel budget must therefore be carefully staged. The company should not assume it can hire five strong full-time specialists for two years within the remaining personnel ceiling without contract revenue contributing to payroll.

## 9. Revenue, reporting and ROI framework

### Revenue principles

- The Year-1 baseline must use only executed contracts and cash-collection terms.
- The three existing clients may be described as more than ₹1 crore of management-confirmed opportunity until exact contract values and schedules are supplied; no exact number may be invented.
- Academy revenue is excluded from Year-1 downside and base cases.
- Managed-FDE revenue is included only after a signed paid client requirement.
- Every engagement must have scope, fees, delivery effort, client acceptance, invoice milestone, collection date, and contribution-margin tracking.

### Required scenarios

| Scenario | Included revenue | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Downside | Signed work only; no new sales; no academy or FDE revenue | Tests survival and cash runway |
| Base case | Signed work plus qualified-pipeline conversions approved by the Board | Tests the operating plan without speculative income |
| Upside | Base case plus paid managed-FDE client conversion | Shows scalability only after market proof |

### Monthly investor MIS

The CEO will circulate, within the agreed reporting deadline, opening cash, tranche funds received, payroll, delivery cost, office cost, sales cost, revenue invoiced, cash collected, receivables ageing, gross margin, monthly burn, and months of runway. The pack must also list signed pipeline, client-delivery status, hiring, material risks, and decisions required from the Board.

### ROI statement

No fixed return, dividend, valuation, or exit timeline is promised. Investor return may arise through profitable operations, dividends only after sustainable cash generation and working-capital needs are met, and growth in equity value. Any ROI percentage requires the final cap table, funding instrument, company valuation, contract-backed financial model, tax treatment, and future dilution assumptions.

## 10. Principal risks and mitigating actions

| Risk | Why it matters | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| Generic services positioning | The market is crowded and price-sensitive | Sell defined packages, use case studies, track job profitability, and choose an initial vertical after client research |
| Client concentration | Three early clients may create dependency | Set a client-concentration threshold and build a documented pipeline beyond redirected clients |
| Unverified client transfer | Parambhaav work may not automatically belong to the new company | Obtain client consent, contracts, and documented transfer/novation terms before counting revenue |
| CEO/key-person dependency | Prashanth is currently the only identified technical person | Hire/contract a senior technical lead and document delivery, code, security, and client processes |
| Salary and fixed-cost burden | ₹60 lakh CEO compensation is material against the capital envelope | Stage hiring, use contractors selectively, and maintain Board review of burn and cash runway |
| Academy demand and reputation | Training is competitive and outcomes can be disputed | Pilot in Year 2; separate P&L; no placement guarantee; use transparent policies and assessments |
| FDE delivery risk | Junior talent cannot safely substitute for senior client-facing delivery | Deploy only senior-led, company-managed resources against paid client demand |
| Scope creep and slow collections | Services businesses can lose margin and cash | Use SOWs, change requests, acceptance milestones, advance/milestone billing, and receivables escalation |
